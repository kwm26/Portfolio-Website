Case study · Athletic Training Portal

Turning phone video into feedback athletes can act on.

ATP measures how athletes move from ordinary phone video: joint angles, range of motion, and left-versus-right symmetry, tracked over time. I designed and built the landing page, the athlete mobile view, and a real-time visualization system that draws movement data directly onto video.

01The problem

Measuring how an athlete moves usually takes specialized equipment. ATP does it from video shot on an ordinary phone, which makes the analysis far more accessible but puts all the pressure on the interface. The raw output is dense: angles at every tracked joint, range of motion, left-versus-right comparisons, all changing frame by frame and session over session.

And it has to work for different people. Coaches need a team-wide read on who needs attention. Athletes need feedback on their own movement that they can actually act on. Clinicians need to see a joint drifting toward injury, and then track its way back during rehab.

02My role

As Chief Experience Officer, I coordinated and developed ATP's landing page and athlete mobile view, and I lead our marketing strategy team. I also built the real-time visualization system that renders performance metrics and biomechanical data directly onto live training video.

03From video to skeleton

The first thing the product has to earn is trust. Showing the original clip next to the tracked skeleton lets anyone check the system's read of the movement with their own eyes before they look at a single number.

06Real-time visualization system

I built a modular visualization system for real-time athlete monitoring during training, using Python, OpenCV, and PIL. It renders performance metrics and biomechanical data directly onto live video. A widget-based rendering engine keeps each UI component separate and scalable, NumPy broadcasting handles gradient rendering, and exponential moving-average smoothing keeps the numbers from jittering.

From 7 FPS to 60+ FPS. Frame rate was the biggest hurdle. I used AI-assisted ideation to find optimization strategies and took rendering from the 7 FPS I started with to over 60 FPS.
